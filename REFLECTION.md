## The person I would want to become

In a world where personal agents can produce research, write programs and support business decisions, I would want to become a person whose judgment other people can trust. Access to an answer would make the choice of questions, the quality of evidence and the consequences of action more important. I would want to explain how I reached a conclusion, identify the assumptions behind it and take responsibility for the decisions that follow. In a financial setting, this would include understanding who bears a risk and whether a technically attractive decision fits that person's circumstances.

## Ambitions that would continue to matter

My ambitions would include developing sound financial reasoning, building useful systems and earning the confidence of the people who rely on my work. An agent could accelerate the preparation of an analysis while the purpose of that analysis would still require my attention. I would judge a project by whether it helps someone make a better decision, whether its limitations are clear and whether its benefits justify its costs. Learning would continue to matter because understanding a subject helps me notice errors, challenge convenient assumptions and choose an appropriate level of reliance on an automated result.

## Qualities beyond professional output

I would want people to remember my honesty, patience and willingness to listen, particularly when their needs differ from the problem I initially expected to solve. Reliable relationships require time, attention and consistent behavior across ordinary situations. I would value being someone who follows through on a commitment, acknowledges uncertainty and gives other people credit for their contributions. These qualities would shape how I work with colleagues and how I participate in the lives of friends and family, giving my identity a basis that extends across changes in tools and occupations.

## Effort I would choose to keep

I would continue to put effort into understanding difficult ideas, developing physical skills and having conversations that require care. Working through a problem can reveal the limits of my understanding and improve the questions I can ask an agent later. Exercise and direct experience can also provide satisfaction through participation and gradual improvement. I would use assistance deliberately so that efficiency supports activities I care about, while preserving opportunities to practise the abilities and relationships I want to develop.

## Abilities and experiences that would remain valuable

I would invest in careful reasoning, communication, financial knowledge and the ability to coordinate people with different interests. I would also seek direct experience of how organizations work, because decisions involve practical constraints, incentives and effects that a polished output can leave unclear. This homework provides a small example: a plausible answer can depend on the wrong company, a different year or an unsuitable source. Learning to check those conditions develops habits that also matter when a future agent produces more complicated recommendations.

## How I would define myself

I would define myself through the commitments I choose, the people I care about and the standards I maintain when evaluating my own actions. Productivity would remain useful as one measure of progress toward a worthwhile purpose. I would also ask whether I acted fairly, learned something important and respected the people affected by my decisions. This approach would allow my sense of identity to develop alongside new technology while retaining a clear connection to my values and responsibilities.

## What I can start changing tomorrow

I can begin by keeping a short decision journal that records my question, the evidence I used, the assumptions I accepted and the outcome I expected. When using AI for study or programming, I can identify one important claim to check independently and explain the result in my own words. I can also reserve regular time for exercise, relationships and learning through direct experience. Reviewing these habits each week would help me assess whether the assistance I use is supporting the person I want to become.
