# Reflection: judgment, responsibility and learning from Homework 4

Author: eula | Student: SANG Yuchen | Student ID: 1155262204

This reflection connects Homework 4 with my education and experiences and was developed with AI assistance, as permitted in Task 6.

Homework 4 gave me a practical way to consider the future described in Task 6, because answering questions about financial documents required careful decisions about how information was organized, selected and checked. A financial figure needed its company, year and table labels to remain meaningful, and the answer depended on whether the system found the relevant evidence. This connects with my finance and financial technology studies and with my internship at uSMART Securities, where I worked on receipt processing and reimbursement automation and helped improve an assistant for testing trading strategies. Across these activities, I see a continuing need for people who understand both the financial question and the information used to answer it. If capable personal agents become widely available over the next ten years, I would want to become a financial professional whose judgment colleagues can trust. My ambition would remain to develop useful financial tools, with responsibility for choosing worthwhile problems, explaining their assumptions and understanding the consequences of the decisions they support.

The experiments in this assignment also give me a concrete example of how I would want to approach professional responsibility. Converting tables into more detailed text initially reduced the number of correct answers from 32 to 23 out of 60, even though preserving table information seemed useful. Later changes showed how retaining company and section information and selecting evidence more carefully helped the system answer the questions. The final version answered all 60 public questions correctly, which demonstrates its performance on that sample and leaves performance on unfamiliar documents to be examined. These results remind me to explain what an improvement actually achieves and to examine evidence when an apparently sensible change performs poorly. My consulting experience at Deloitte involved organizing findings from interviews, workshops and questionnaires, while my hospital procurement experience required careful document handling and coordination with others. I would want to apply the same care to AI-supported financial work and be known for honest explanations, keeping commitments and consideration for the people affected by a recommendation. Those qualities would continue to matter to me alongside professional achievement.

The effort I would choose to preserve is the effort needed to understand a result well enough to take responsibility for using it. In this assignment, a useful explanation of an incorrect answer required checking which source passages had been selected and whether they contained the company, year and financial item requested. Even when an agent can write code and produce an experiment table, I would still want to practise reading the evidence, asking why a change helped and recognizing what the available information can establish. My earlier research on cryptocurrency trading volumes also involved preparing data before interpreting the analysis, which gives me another reason to value patience and careful reasoning. I would continue developing these abilities together with communication, curiosity and attention to other people's needs. Photography, writing and my experience creating an AI-assisted comic about student stress also matter to my identity, because they allow me to develop personal interests and consider experiences beyond financial work. I would want my relationships, commitments and conduct to remain meaningful parts of how I define myself as technology changes my professional capabilities.

Beginning tomorrow, I can turn these intentions into habits by keeping a brief record of the question, supporting evidence and assumptions behind an important analysis or decision. For a project like Homework 4, I would record the purpose of each change, compare its result with the previous version and inspect a few original passages before trusting a summary of the outcome. When a result looks unusually strong, I would also ask which new documents or questions could help establish how well it works on unfamiliar material. When I use AI to help with code or financial analysis, I can explain the expected behaviour in my own words and check that the output follows from the source information. I can also seek feedback on whether my explanations help another person understand the reasoning and make an informed choice. Alongside these study and work habits, I would reserve regular time for creative activities and attentive conversations with the people I care about. These actions would allow me to assess my progress through the reliability of my decisions, the quality of my relationships and the consistency of the values I put into practice.
