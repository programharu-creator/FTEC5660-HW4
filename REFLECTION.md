# Reflection: the kind of financial professional and person I want to become

Author: eula | Student: SANG Yuchen | Student ID: 1155262204

This reflection draws on my education and experiences and was developed with AI assistance, as permitted in Task 6.

Moving from undergraduate finance study to a master's programme in financial technology has made the future described in this assignment directly relevant to my own ambitions. During my internship at uSMART Securities, I worked on an automated receipt processing and reimbursement workflow and helped improve an assistant for testing trading strategies. These projects gave me practical experience of using AI in activities that involve financial information and decisions, where the usefulness of a system depends on the problem it addresses, the reliability of its inputs and the way people use its output. If powerful agents become widely available over the next ten years, I would still want to develop financial judgment and build systems that people can use with confidence. I would want colleagues to trust me to ask whether a proposed improvement is appropriate, examine its assumptions and explain its consequences. My ambition to combine finance and technology would therefore continue, with more attention to choosing worthwhile problems and taking responsibility for the decisions made with the tools I help develop.

My consulting and procurement experiences also shaped how I understand that responsibility. At Deloitte, I participated in research for a digital transformation project through workshops, interviews and questionnaires, then helped organize the findings into recommendations and implementation ideas. The process depended on understanding the concerns of people doing different kinds of work and deciding which issues deserved attention. During my internship in a hospital procurement centre, I coordinated with suppliers, reviewed documents and maintained records, following procedures that allowed other people to examine the decisions and supporting information. In a future workplace with capable agents, I would want to be known for listening carefully, keeping commitments and treating the people affected by a decision fairly. An agent could prepare an analysis or organize documents quickly, while the choice of whose concerns to consider and how to respond would remain part of my own responsibility. I would continue developing communication, practical judgment and the patience to understand a situation before recommending a solution.

My interests outside technical work also matter to the person I hope to become. Attending the IMF and World Bank Annual Meetings as a student representative and taking part in international organization training programmes introduced me to discussions about development, cooperation and the effects of economic decisions on different communities. These experiences encourage me to consider the broader purpose of professional work alongside the interests of an individual organization. Photography and writing offer another way to practise attention, develop a personal perspective and express concerns that matter to me. Creating an AI-assisted comic about the everyday stress experienced by university students required me to consider students' experiences alongside the technical choices involved, and it gives me a useful example of the kind of purpose I would want technology to support. Beyond professional achievements, I would want to be known for curiosity, consideration and a willingness to contribute to other people's understanding. I would define myself through those commitments, the relationships I maintain and the standards I apply to my own choices as my professional abilities and tools develop.

The effort I would choose to keep is the effort that develops my understanding and my ability to act responsibly. My research on cryptocurrency trading volumes involved collecting and organizing data before carrying out analysis, and I would continue practising the financial reasoning needed to judge the meaning and limits of such results even when an agent could generate the calculations. I would also preserve time for learning through direct experience, developing creative interests and having conversations that require attention. Beginning tomorrow, I can make these intentions practical by recording the question, evidence and assumptions behind an important decision, then checking its outcome later. When AI helps me write code or analyze financial information, I can first explain the expected result in my own words and verify a key claim against the source material. I can ask for feedback on how clearly I communicate a recommendation and reserve regular time for photography, writing and the people I care about. These habits would help me judge my progress through the quality of my decisions, the consistency of my conduct and the contribution I make to others.
